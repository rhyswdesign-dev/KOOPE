## 0. The One-Sentence Answer

**KŌOPE's customer is the person for whom a drink has become a decision instead of a habit** — they drink less than they did three years ago, spend more per bottle, host at home instead of defaulting to bars, treat taste as identity, and are standing in front of a shelf, a cart, or a Friday night with no one to ask.

Everything below is that person at different ages, incomes, and moments.

## 1. Segment Architecture — three rings

```
RING 1 — THE ENGINE (build for them first)
  P1 The Weeknight Maker      → retention, Made It, KŌOPE+ core
  P2 The First-Time Host      → conversion at the strongest desire peak
  P4 The Aisle Premiumizer    → scan volume, the data engine, SEO demand

RING 2 — THE MULTIPLIERS (free users who bring users)
  P3 The Anxious Gift Buyer   → December acquisition, Want-list registry
  P5 The Gen Z Curator        → shares, Wrapped, cultural gravity

RING 3 — THE LATER TIERS (proven audiences, priced separately)
  P6 The Collector            → $99/yr add-on (Phase 4, Collector Blueprint)

```

Ring 1 pays the bills and feeds the flywheel. Ring 2 costs nothing and compounds. Ring 3 is real money but only after the data proves the audience mix — the Master Plan's Phase 4 gate.

## 2. The Personas — full depth

### P1 · Maya, 31 — The Weeknight Maker

_"You already own tonight's drink."_

**Snapshot.** Product marketing manager at a mid-size SaaS company, Toronto. $95K CAD. Lives with her partner (a nurse who works shifts) in a one-bedroom-plus-den in Leslieville. No kids; a cat. Hybrid work — office Tuesday–Thursday downtown, home Monday/Friday.

**Work life & daily rhythm.** Slack all day, two "real" meetings, gym at lunch twice a week. Commutes by streetcar with headphones — podcasts, not music. Evenings are hers three nights a week because of her partner's shift schedule; the drink she makes at 7:30pm on those nights is a _ritual of decompression_, not consumption. One drink, made properly, is the line between the workday and her actual life.

**Where she lives & why it matters.** Leslieville / east-end Toronto: walkable, café-dense, LCBO within 10 minutes on foot. Rents. Her kitchen is small, so her "bar" is one cabinet shelf plus four nice glasses — space constraint makes _versatility per bottle_ her real currency, which is exactly the Bar Optimizer's promise.

**Where she goes.** Farmers' market Saturday morning (ritual, not necessity). One cocktail bar she considers "hers" (Civil Liberties-type — bartender's choice, no menu). Pilates studio. A friend's place or hers every second weekend. Two big trips a year (Portugal last year; Oaxaca next — she came back from Mexico _with a mezcal opinion_, which is how half her category interests start: travel first, aisle second).

**Shopping habits & frequency.** Groceries 2×/week (one big shop, one top-up). LCBO roughly **every 2–3 weeks, basket $45–90** — one "restock" bottle and occasionally one "curiosity" bottle. The curiosity bottle is where she stands in the aisle for six minutes reading a label that tells her nothing. She researches >$50 purchases on her phone _in the store_, currently via a chaotic mix of Reddit and Google.

**Where the money goes.** Rent 32% of income. Then, in order: travel fund, food (restaurants ~3×/month, deliberately good ones), fitness, home objects (she'll pay $60 for the right water carafe), spirits/wine ~$120–160/month total. Owns maybe 9 bottles; three she loves, four she regrets, two are vermouth dying slowly in the cupboard (she doesn't know it should be refrigerated — a contextual micro-lesson waiting to happen).

**What she drinks.** 3–5 drinks/week, down from ~8 three years ago — and she'd tell you that proudly. Negroni variations, highballs, a good daiquiri in summer. Wine with dinner out. One NA night mid-week — she keeps a zero-proof aperitif in the fridge and doesn't consider it a compromise. Whisky-curious but intimidated: the wall of Scotch is where she feels most like KŌOPE's user.

**Friend group.** Core group of 6–8 from university plus work-adjacent friends. The group's social calendar is _dinner-party shaped_ now — bars are for catching up one-on-one; groups gather at homes. She's the one who "knows drinks" in her group by default, which she finds both flattering and stressful — her reputation exceeds her actual knowledge, and she knows it. **That gap is the product.**

**Hosting & parties.** Hosts 1–2×/month, 4–8 people, "dinner that becomes a long evening." Makes one batch cocktail when hosting (learned the hard way not to play bartender all night). Her anxieties, in order: will there be enough, will the one drink land, what does she serve the friend who's not drinking, does the table look like she tried. She screenshots recipes and loses them. Her party spend: $80–140 a night, and she'd spend $20 more for the night to feel _handled_.

**Phone & app life.** Instagram (lurker), Pinterest (boards for everything), Strava, NYT Cooking (paid — the proof she pays for utility that respects her), Spotify. She has downloaded and deleted two cocktail apps: "felt like a database wearing an app."

**The KŌOPE moment.** First contact: scans the mezcal she brought back from Oaxaca to find out what to do with it — Answer Card says _"you can make 7 drinks with what you own."_ Second session: Tuesday, "What Can I Make." Converts to KŌOPE+ the third time she hits the greyed-out 4th recipe **or** the first time she hosts with the app — whichever comes first. Annual plan, doesn't think twice at $59.99 (it's one cocktail out, per year).

**Lifetime shape.** High retention if the Made It loop lands; she's the North Star metric embodied — a **Weekly Maker**. Churn risk: the app going quiet (nothing new to make with her shelf) or feeling gamified-at (streaks, confetti — she will screenshot it mockingly to the group chat if the app congratulates her like a toddler; the Bible's weekly-cadence rule exists for her).

**Reach her via:** SEO bottle pages (she googles bottles in-aisle), the one cocktail bar's bartender (borrowed trust), Pinterest-native content, a friend's Guest Menu link.

### P2 · Jordan, 28 — The First-Time Host

_"Friday, handled."_

**Snapshot.** Software engineer (3 years in), Austin, TX. $130K. Rents a two-bedroom with one roommate in Mueller. Single, dating. Moved to Austin at 24 knowing nobody; his social life is _built_, not inherited — which makes hosting strategically important to him in a way it never was for his parents.

**Work life & rhythm.** Remote-first, office optional. His week has no natural social architecture (no commute, no colleagues down the hall), so he manufactures it: climbing gym Tuesdays, a run club he joined explicitly to meet people, and — increasingly — having people over. Discretionary income is high; discretionary _confidence_ is not.

**Where he lives & why it matters.** Austin is the archetype metro: young, transplant-heavy, home-entertaining culture (backyards, cheap-ish square footage relative to coasts, BYO norms), a Total Wine within a 10-minute drive. His equivalents live in Nashville, Denver, Raleigh, Columbus — the _transplant cities_ where nobody has a family basement bar to inherit and everybody is building a social graph from zero.

**Where he goes.** Climbing gym, run club, two breweries (the default group hang), a cocktail bar for dates only — $17 drinks make it an occasion, not a habit, which is exactly the UK/US on-trade data in one person. Whole Foods and H-E-B. Total Wine **monthly-ish, $60–120**, plus panic runs before hosting.

**Where the money goes.** Rent, a car, climbing gear, flights to weddings (four this year — he's 28), dating, and a slowly accumulating apartment: he bought a real couch last year, a record player, decent glasses. The home bar is the _next_ apartment upgrade in his head — it's a masculinity-coded version of nesting, and he'd never phrase it that way.

**What he drinks.** Beer socially, bourbon neat as his "order," margaritas and espresso martinis when out with dates. 4–7 drinks/week. Knows he likes "smoky" but couldn't say why. Owns 6 bottles, four of which were gifts or party leftovers. His knowledge source is TikTok and one impressive friend.

**Friend group.** Concentric circles: run club (12 loose ties), climbing crew (5), two close friends from college, plus dates. Hosting is how loose ties become close ones — his actual motive. The group is BYO-culture by default; being the guy with _a bar and a plan_ is visible social capital.

**Hosting & parties.** Has hosted 3 times, all "people over to watch something" with beer. Wants to throw the version with cocktails and has bailed twice — the math (how much of what?), the sequencing (I can't shake drinks and talk), and the fear of it reading as try-hard. **He is the person the Hosting Planner's batch calculator and prep timeline were built for.** First party free (≤4 guests) is his exact on-ramp; "people coming Friday" is his paywall, and he'll pay monthly first ($7.99 feels like nothing; $59.99 annual feels like a commitment to a self he isn't yet).

**Phone & app life.** TikTok (heavy), Reddit, Strava, Hinge, YouTube tutorials for everything he doesn't know. He will watch a 90-second "how to batch old fashioneds for 8" video the night before. He shares wins: if the party lands, the Guest Menu link and the morning-after group chat _are_ the acquisition loop.

**The KŌOPE moment.** Finds it via TikTok or a guest at someone else's party ("the menu had a QR"). First scan is at Total Wine holding a $55 bourbon: _is this actually good or just marketed at me?_ — value-on-scan wins him instantly. Converts the Thursday before his first real cocktail party. **His Guest Menu is worth 1–3 installs per party** (the Spread metrics row exists to prove this).

**Churn risk:** hosting is seasonal for him (fall/winter/playoffs); the weeknight Maker loop has to catch him between parties or he laps. Tonight's Pick and the fading scaffolds are his retention story.

**Reach him via:** TikTok/short video (the batch-math demo), run-club word of mouth, Guest Menu QR codes at parties he attends.

### P3 · Susan, 54 — The Anxious Gift Buyer

_"The bottle they actually want."_

**Snapshot.** School district administrator, Naperville, IL (suburban Chicago). Married, two adult kids, one son-in-law. Household income $160K. **Drinks almost nothing herself** — a glass of wine at holidays. She is KŌOPE's most important _non-drinker_.

**Work life & rhythm.** Structured, calendared, competent — she runs logistics for a living and is the family's chief gift officer: 20+ meaningful gifts a year (kids, spouses' colleagues, teachers, her boss, the neighbor who snowblows their driveway). Gifts are her love language and her project management.

**Where she lives & why it matters.** Suburbia: Binny's and Costco within 15 minutes, everything by car. The liquor megastore is the single most disorienting retail environment she enters all year — 40-foot whiskey aisles, zero interpretation, staff who talk past her. She is the _most anxious person in the aisle_ (Master Plan §2.2), holding a $70 decision about someone else's taste.

**Shopping habits.** Costco weekly, Target, Amazon for everything schedulable. Bottle shopping: **3–5 times a year, $40–120 per bottle, entirely occasion-driven** — December (three bottles), a retirement, a wedding, a thank-you. Research method today: texting her daughter "what does Mike like again?" and a Google search that returns affiliate listicles.

**Where the money goes.** Mortgage nearly done, college done; money now flows to experiences (a big family trip), home projects, and generosity. She's price-_aware_, not price-_constrained_: she'll happily spend $85 if she is *certain* it's right, and $40 if she's guessing. Certainty is the product.

**What she drinks.** Functionally NA. This matters: **gift mode must require zero spirits literacy** — she doesn't know rye from bourbon and doesn't want a lesson, she wants a verdict. "Scanning for someone else?" → _"Great gift for a bourbon drinker who likes sweeter profiles. Fair price: $65–75."_ That sentence is her entire relationship with the app, and it's enough.

**Friend group & social life.** Church, a walking group, book club, couples' dinners where the men talk about whiskey and she takes mental notes for December. Her network is _dense and older_ — and when gift mode works once, she tells the walking group. Suburban word-of-mouth among gift-buying women is slow, cheap, and permanent.

**Hosting & parties.** Hosts constantly — holidays for 14, graduation parties, showers — but her hosting is food-centered; drinks are "what people bring." She will never subscribe. **She is not a revenue user; she is an acquisition channel and a data source** (gift-mode scans are scan-context gold: SKU + price seen + "gift" flag).

**The KŌOPE moment.** Her daughter (a Maya) airdrops her the app in November: "just scan bottles and it'll tell you if Mike would like it." She uses it four times in December, gets four verdicts, nails Christmas for the first time in years, and the son-in-law's Want-list (the registry loop) makes next year _automatic_. **Want-list-as-registry converts her from guesser to fulfiller — the single highest-relief product moment we can ship, and it ships before Q4 (Campaign: The Handoff).**

**Reach her via:** her adult kids (in-app "share your Want-list with family" prompts), December SEO ("gift for bourbon lover"), local-news-style holiday gift content. Never via TikTok.

**Friend group.** University friends scattered across London — the group chat is active daily, meetings are quarterly and treasured. New-parent friends from NCT class locally. Dinner parties of two couples are the social unit; someone always brings a bottle _chosen to be talked about_. The bottle-as-conversation-piece is his gifting _and_ his hosting behavior at once.

**Hosting & parties.** Hosts couples' dinners monthly; the drink moment is one good cocktail before dinner or the whisky after. Low volume, high ceremony. Won't use a "party planner" — but **Make It Anyway is his paid feature** (he owns 14 bottles; the recipe calls for the 15th) and cellar/collection value view flatters an instinct he'd deny having. The Answer Card's sourced range earns trust visit one; "unlocks 11 recipes with your shelf" surprises him ("it knows my shelf?") visit three. He is also a **future Collector-lite** — the £200 sherry-cask bottle in the cupboard makes him Blueprint-curious without self-identifying as a collector.

**Reach him via:** SEO bottle pages (his in-aisle googling _is_ the channel), Reddit presence-without-marketing, indie bottle shop partnerships (a shelf QR: "scan any bottle").

**Reach him via:** the bourbon groups (organic, via genuinely correct valuations), r/bourbon, distillery-town SEO ("what is Weller 12 worth"), never lifestyle marketing.

Industry bars after shift (the 1am economy), other people's menus on her nights off (competitive research as leisure), Tales of the Cocktail once (self-funded, transformative), brand-sponsored trainings (the free education economy brands run — note: **brands already pay to reach her**, which previews the B2B layer).

**Why she matters before Year 2:** three Amaras doing drops = borrowed audiences, credibility with Chloe and Maya, and the supply-side handshake toward the trade graph (Master Plan Phase 4 / endgame). ### Case Study 1 — The Tuesday Negroni (Maya · retention engine)

Tuesday, 7:40pm, partner on nights. Maya opens KŌOPE out of the habit the app built in week two: **Tonight's Pick** says _Negroni Sbagliato — you have everything, 4 minutes._ She makes it, taps **Made It** (her 11th), and the recipe card shows slightly less detail than last time — the fading scaffold. She notices she didn't need the measurements anyway and feels the small, private pride of _actually knowing how to make a drink now._ A micro-lesson surfaces post-make: "your sweet vermouth lives longer in the fridge." She moves the bottle, mildly annoyed nobody told her sooner.
**What the business sees:** a Weekly Maker week-11 retention event, a Made It log feeding the North Star, a shelf graph one bottle-state richer.
**What she'd tell a friend:** "it's the only app that knows what's in my cabinet."

### Case Study 2 — Six People on Friday (Jordan · conversion at the desire peak)

Wednesday night. Jordan has six people coming Friday — the run club, first time at his place, including someone he likes. He opens KŌOPE to figure out _how much bourbon for eight old fashioneds_ and hits the Hosting Planner: menu of 2 cocktails + 1 NA, batch math, a shopping list ($74 at Total Wine), a prep timeline that says *batch Thursday night, 10 minutes Friday.* The plan is exactly one screen deep — and gated. **"People coming Friday" fires at the peak: he subscribes monthly without leaving the flow.** Friday works. The girl he likes scans the QR on the Guest Menu to see what's in her drink. Saturday morning she has the app.
**What the business sees:** a desire-peak conversion (never a curiosity-peak), a $74 party-cart affiliate opportunity, one guest→install.
**What he'd tell a friend:** nothing — the party _is_ the telling.

### Case Study 3 — The £68 Bottle (Dev · the aisle wedge, free forever)

Saturday, indie bottle shop, toddler asleep in the pram (a 9-minute window). Dev picks up a heavily-awarded Speyside at £68. Scans it. **Answer Card, 2.4 seconds:** identity, honest flavor notes, _fair price £45–52 — you're seeing £68_, source cited. He puts it down, scans the shelf neighbor: £54, fair range £50–58, _"unlocks 9 recipes with your shelf."_ He buys it, and stands in the shop for one more minute reading what he can now make. He screenshots the first card to the group chat with "obscene markup, avoid."
**What the business sees:** two scan-context events (store, prices seen, one pass, one conversion) — the data brands will pay for; a screenshot that markets value-on-scan better than we ever could; DRAM's wedge deleted in the wild.
**What he'd tell a friend:** he already did.

### Case Study 4 — December 18th (Susan · the gifting chain)

Binny's, six days before Christmas, three gifts to solve. Susan opens the app her daughter installed for her, taps **"scanning for someone else?"**, and scans a bourbon for son-in-law Mike. _"Strong match for Mike's list — he's marked this distillery as a favorite. Fair price $67–75."_ Mike's Want-list — shared to the family in November — turns her three-store guessing tour into an 11-minute errand. She buys all three bottles in one aisle. On Christmas morning Mike is visibly, genuinely surprised; Susan mentions the app to her sister that afternoon, and to the walking group in January.
**What the business sees:** three gift-context scans, a Want-list registry loop closed (the Handoff campaign's core motion), a zero-CAC household acquired through a non-drinker.
**What she'd tell a friend:** "it's how I finally got Mike right."

### Case Study 5 — The Amaro Screenshot (Chloe · the culture loop)

Saturday, a friend's apartment, a strange bottle on the counter. Chloe scans it mid-party: _Amaro Nonino — grappa-based, orange and honey, made by four generations of one family._ She retells the story to three people within the hour (knowledge as social currency) and adds it to **Want**. Two weeks later she buys it ($42, her bottle of the month), hosts "amaro night" for the flat, and posts the share card — which links to a web landing page, where two people she's never met tap through to the App Store. In December, her **Wrapped** says her year was _"amaro-forward, mezcal-curious, 31 drinks made, taste twin: your roommate."_ It's on her story in 40 seconds.
**What the business sees:** share-card→web→install (the loop, not the dead-end export), a taste profile deepening, December's tentpole doing its job.
**What she'd tell a friend:** the story about the four generations. The app rides along.

### Case Study 6 — The Spreadsheet Funeral (Rob · the Phase-4 add-on)

Sunday night, basement. Rob's spreadsheet says he paid $2,340 for the shelf; a Facebook thread says his Weller is "worth $200, maybe $260, depends." He scans it in KŌOPE: *market range $210–245, two sources, updated monthly.* He scans 29 more bottles over two beers. **What he'd tell a friend:** "the numbers are actually sourced."

### Case Study 7 — One Bottle, Four Personas (the flywheel in miniature)

A single SKU — a mid-shelf, well-made reposado at $52 — moves through the graph in one quarter: **Dev** scans it in-aisle (value: fair), passes, but the scan logs a compare-context. **Maya** scans it at home after Oaxaca, keeps it in Owned, makes three Palomas (Made It ×3). **Chloe** meets it at Maya's table, Wants it, buys it, shares it. **Susan** buys it in December because it's on Chloe's Want-list. Four scans, three contexts (store, home, gift), two conversions, one Made-It stream — and the brand behind the reposado can, next quarter, buy an honest aggregate report saying exactly this happened 4,000 times, without ever buying a single recommendation slot.
**This is the covenant-bound business model in one bottle.**

## 5. Cross-Persona Patterns — the shared truths

**What every Ring 1–2 persona has in common (the actual target definition):**

1. **Consideration inflation.** They all drink less than 3 years ago and spend more per decision — the market research's core finding is the personas' one shared trait.
2. **Home is the venue.** Every one of them shifted social gravity from bars to homes; the bar became an occasion, the home became the default. Hosting is the new third place.
3. **Taste as identity.** From Chloe's bio-level curation to Rob's basement shelf to Dev's design-decision bottles — the drink says who they are. (Strava/Wrapped mechanics work on all of them; streaks work on none.)
4. **Nobody to ask.** The bartender knowledge-transfer died with visit frequency; the shelf doesn't talk; Reddit is homework. Every persona hit the same wall: _high-stakes decision, zero guidance at the moment of decision._
5. **They pay for utility that respects them.** NYT Cooking, Strava, the Athletic, Beli, Monzo — this cohort demonstrably subscribes to well-made single-purpose tools at $50–100/yr. KŌOPE+ at $59.99 sits exactly in their proven band.

## 6. Validation Plan — how these composites earn or lose canon status

These personas are hypotheses with confidence levels, not facts. The Phase 1 instruments adjudicate:

1. **Scan-context distribution** (store/home/gift) tells us the real Dev:Maya:Susan ratio within weeks of the Answer Card v2 shipping.
2. **Made It week-1 rate** — the Master Plan's "this number decides everything" — is specifically the _Maya hypothesis test._ If new users scan but don't make, the center of gravity is Dev (utility/reference) not Maya (ritual), and the retention roadmap reweights toward value-on-scan depth and the collector path.
3. **First-party interviews:** 8–10 conversations per Ring 1 persona (recruit from early users at the moment they exhibit the behavior — e.g., interview someone the week they first use the Hosting Planner). One founder-day per persona per quarter.
4. **Paywall-peak attribution:** every conversion tags which of the three peaks fired. If "people coming Friday" outconverts the greyed 4th recipe, Jordan outranks Maya and Hosting earns its tab earlier.
5. **Kill rule:** any persona that hasn't shown up in the data by Phase 2 exit gets demoted from this doc — the same discipline the Master Plan applies to features.
